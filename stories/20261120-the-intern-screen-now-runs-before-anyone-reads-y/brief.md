## Reader

A first- or second-year computer science student applying for software engineering internships, probably for the first or second time. They have a résumé they have polished more than once and a vague plan to "do LeetCode later". After reading, they will know where an automated assessment now sits in a typical application, why that changes what to prepare first, and how to split the next four to six weeks between the résumé, the online assessment and projects they can talk about.

## Angle

For many Summer 2027 internships the first gate is an automated coding assessment, not a recruiter reading your résumé, so the sensible order of preparation is assessment readiness first, a résumé that is good enough second, and polishing last.

## Outline

**Opening (no heading).** Start from a concrete scene: a student on their fifth résumé revision while an assessment link sits unopened in their inbox. State the point by the end of the opening: the order of the funnel changed, so the order of preparation should too.

## The funnel got reordered
Explain the shift in plain terms: application, automated assessment, then résumé review by a person for those who pass. Note that this is not universal and varies by company size and type.

## Why the résumé still matters, just later
Avoid overcorrecting. The résumé still decides interviews once you pass the test, and some firms (especially smaller ones) still read first. The claim is about sequencing, not abandonment.

## What the assessment is testing now
Cover the reported redesign around AI use: timed conditions, proctoring or webcam checks, questions that reward explaining a solution, not only producing one. Be explicit about which details are reported versus confirmed.

- [[HUMAN: What did the assessments you took this cycle actually look like? Format, length, whether AI use was banned or monitored, and what surprised you. Only first-hand details.]]

## A preparation order that fits the new funnel
Give the reordered plan as a short, genuinely parallel list (at most 5 items): get to "assessment-ready" on core data structures under time pressure; make the résumé clear and correct, then stop; pick one or two projects you can explain in depth; apply in batches so tests do not cluster; review each assessment within a day. Name the trade-off: less time polishing means some applications go out with a merely good résumé.

- [[HUMAN: Your opinion, with reasons: what would you tell a younger student to cut from their preparation, based on what you saw this season? Include one thing you wasted time on.]]

## Ending: the question worth keeping
Land on one decision: before the next edit to your CV, ask whether you could pass the test that decides if anyone reads it. Short, no recap.
