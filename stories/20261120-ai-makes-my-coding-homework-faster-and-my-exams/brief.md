## Reader

A computer science undergraduate who uses AI coding assistants for assignments and has noticed, or is starting to suspect, that the speed does not carry into exam halls, oral defences or proctored coding tests. Secondary reader: a student about to start a heavy technical semester who wants a study system that still works when the assistant is switched off. After reading, they will be able to separate work that AI can safely speed up from practice that has to stay unassisted, and change one part of their weekly study routine accordingly.

## Angle

AI assistance raises the score on the task in front of you without reliably raising what you can do alone, and as courses move assessment back into rooms without assistants, a student's study system has to treat unassisted practice as the real work and AI as the shortcut it is.

## Outline

**Opening (no heading).** Start from the writer's own contrast this semester: coursework that felt quick with an assistant, and an exam that felt harder than it should have. State the point by the end: AI improved the output, not necessarily the person producing it, and exam formats are catching up with that.

## Better scores are not the same as learning
Summarise what research says about AI assistance raising performance on the assisted task without matching gains when the help is removed. Cite primary studies only; explain the difference between performance during practice and learning that lasts.

## Debugging code you did not write is its own skill
Explain why reading and fixing AI-generated code under time pressure is harder than writing your own: you did not build the mental model. Connect to the reported difficulty novices have debugging AI output.

## Exams are moving back into the room
Report, carefully sourced, how courses are redesigning assessment: proctored tests, oral defences, in-class coding. Keep the claim as strong as the source allows.

- [[HUMAN: Which of your own exams or assessments this semester were proctored, oral or handwritten, and how did that change the way you had to prepare? Facts only, no grading details you are not allowed to share.]]

## What I changed in my study system
The practical core. Separate tasks into ones where AI is a reasonable shortcut (boilerplate, looking up syntax, explaining an error message after you have tried) and ones that must stay unassisted (first attempts at algorithms, tracing code by hand, proofs). Suggest a simple rule, such as attempt first, then ask, then redo without help.

- [[HUMAN: The specific habits you changed this semester and whether they helped. For example: a rule about when you open the assistant, practising tracing by hand, timed unassisted attempts. Be honest about what did not work.]]

## Ending
Land on one decision: decide in advance which practice is for learning and keep the assistant out of it. End with a question worth keeping, such as: if the assistant vanished tomorrow, which part of this week's work could you still do?

- [[HUMAN: Optional: one sentence on what you will do differently for your final exams this term.]]

## Claims to verify

- Studies showing AI assistance raises task performance without equivalent learning gains once assistance is removed. Find the primary paper(s); the Springer article in the idea's evidence (International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, DOI 10.1007/s40593-025-00496-4) should be read directly and its actual findings, sample and context reported accurately. A widely cited related study is Bastani et al., "Generative AI Can Harm Learning" (high-school maths, Türkiye); verify details before citing.
- Novice programmers find debugging AI-generated code harder; the evidence link is a news aggregator, so trace it to the original study before citing.
- "Two-thirds of CS educators redesigning exams" in response to AI: the source is an aggregator; find the original survey (publisher, sample size, date) or drop the figure.
- Shift towards oral defences and proctored tests: find at least one named university or course policy, or a reputable news report with a named reporter.
- Any statement about the writer's own institution's assessment policies should come from the official course or university page, not memory.
