# House style (every AI stage reads this; the linter enforces the measurable parts)

`voice.md` says *who* is writing. This file says *how every story is built*, so ten stories read like one writer.

## Story shape

1. **Title** — sentence case, 40–70 characters, a specific claim or question. No colons-plus-listicle ("7 Ways…: The Ultimate Guide").
2. **Subtitle** — one sentence, 80–140 characters, says what the reader gets.
3. **Opening (first 2–4 short paragraphs)** — start from a concrete observation, scene or problem, never a definition or a statistic dump. State the point of the piece by the end of the opening.
4. **Body** — 3–6 sections with `##` headings (Medium "big T"). Use `###` (Medium "small T") sparingly. Nothing deeper; Medium has no H4.
5. **Ending** — one short section that lands the point: a decision, a question worth keeping, or what to do next. No "In conclusion", no recap of every section.

## Paragraphs and sentences

- Paragraphs: 1–4 sentences; never more than ~80 words.
- Sentences: average 12–20 words. Mix lengths. A one-line paragraph is allowed for emphasis, at most 3 per story.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Cut "very", "really", "just", "actually" unless they carry meaning.
- One idea per paragraph. If a paragraph repeats the previous one in other words, delete it.

## Formatting

- Bold: at most one phrase per section, for the key claim. No bold sentences.
- Lists: only for genuinely parallel items (steps, options). Max 7 items. Never a list as a substitute for an argument.
- Pull quote: at most one per story, a line from the story itself.
- Code: fenced blocks with language; keep under 20 lines; explain before showing.
- Links: inline on descriptive words, never "click here". Primary sources over aggregators.
- Numbers: digits for 10+, units spelled out once (e.g. "SGD 900").
- Spelling: British/Singapore English (colour, organise), consistently.
- No emojis in headings or body. No hashtags in text.

## Point of view

- First person singular where the writer is speaking from experience; second person for advice.
- Take a position. Name the trade-off. Say where you are unsure.
- Experience beats authority: show what happened, then generalise.

## AI-tell phrases (never use; linter blocks them)

delve, tapestry, game-changer, unlock, navigate the complexities, in today's fast-paced world, it is important to note, it's worth noting, in conclusion, embark on a journey, ever-evolving, landscape (figurative), realm, testament to, seamless, leverage (as a verb), robust, harness the power, at the end of the day, not just X but Y (as a formula), let's dive in, buckle up.

Also avoid: rhetorical-question openers, triplet lists of adjectives, em dashes more than ~1 per 150 words.
